In Tokyo's prime districts, land is the asset. Buildings are secondary. And land of this scale, in this location, with these protections, is no longer being created. The main residence extends across 830.49 sqm of reinforced concrete across three levels — basement, ground, and first floor — completed March 1999 and designed for a life lived at this scale. A principal living area of over 65 sqm proportioned for formal entertaining. Multiple bedroom suites including a traditional Japanese-style room. ②The Neighbourhood
There are addresses in Tokyo that require no introduction. Minami Aoyama is one of them — and has been for generations. Long regarded as the city's most quietly prestigious residential enclave, it occupies the intersection of cultural depth, architectural distinction, and absolute discretion that Beverly Hills and London's Mayfair are also known for, but in a register that is entirely its own.

Omotesandō — Tokyo's premier luxury avenue, lined with flagship architecture by Tadao Ando, Herzog &amp; de Meuron, and SANAA alongside the global houses — is minutes away on foot. Yet within the estate, the city recedes entirely. That is not a coincidence of geography. It is a consequence of scale.

Omotesandō Station is eight minutes on foot. Gaienmae, eight minutes. The Nezu Museum — one of Tokyo's most respected private collections of East Asian art — is within the immediate neighbourhood. Meiji Jingu Gaien, a historic open park at the heart of the city, is moments away.
