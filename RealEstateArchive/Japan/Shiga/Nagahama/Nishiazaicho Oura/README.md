## Description

■Beyond the Known Lake: A Private Sanctuary upon the Sacred Waters of Oku-Biwa

For four million years, the northernmost reaches of Lake Biwa—a hidden sanctuary known as Oku-Biwa—have been revered as a realm where the deities reside. This is not the lake known to the masses; it is a private world defined by ancient silence, crystalline purity, and profound spiritual depth.

While the lake’s vast shoreline stretches across 235 kilometers, less than 1% remains eligible for development. This villa occupies the crown jewel of that rare fraction: a first-row, freehold estate of nearly 13,000 sqm. Here, there are no neighboring structures—only the eternal, divine dialogue between ancient mountains and the mirror-like water.

■ Architecture — The Philosophy of 'Yohaku'

Designed by award-winning architect Kenzo Tabe, the villa is a physical manifestation of Yohaku—the Zen concept of the 'Meaningful Void.' In this design, the space left empty is more powerful than the structure itself.

- The Architecture of Invisibility: The structure does not compete with the divine landscape; it recedes to allow the presence of the lake to be felt in every breath.
- Material Satori: The warmth of ancient timber meets the stoic clarity of steel, creating a perfect balance between nature and human mindfulness.
- The Ritual of Light: Sunlight is not treated as a utility, but as a guest that drifts through the villa, marking the passage of time as a silent, meditative flow.

--------------------------------------------------
■ Sensory Immersion — The Sound of Absolute Silence

The villa is engineered as a sensory fortress, shielding the spirit from the chaotic noise of the modern world:

- Sacred Purity: A whole-house water purification system that honors the lake’s legendary sanctity and crystal-clear purity.
- Organic Acoustics: A home where silence is not an absence of sound, but a presence of peace—shaped to eliminate all urban friction.

■ Location — Oku-Biwa: The Spiritual Inner Sanctum 

Situated far from the developed southern shores, Oku-Biwa is the spiritual 'inner sanctum' of Japan. Reachable in 10 minutes by limited express from Kyoto Station, it remains a site of profound significance, home to the divine island of Chikubu and immortalized in Zen poetry for over a millennium.

While the southern lake belongs to the world of men, Oku-Biwa offers a metaphysical tranquility that is uniquely Japanese—a landscape where the divine is felt in the stillness of the air.
